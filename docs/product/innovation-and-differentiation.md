## 1. Product overview

IFEXA GO is a local commerce and delivery platform being developed to connect customers, vendors, sourcing agents, and delivery riders.

Its central product concept is an agent-based sourcing workflow. Customers are intended to discover available agents, see the markets they operate in and their proximity where location data is available, request products, and coordinate sourcing, purchasing, and delivery through one platform.

## 2. Problem being addressed

Customers who need products from physical markets may have difficulty identifying the right market, finding someone who can source the product, confirming availability and price, and coordinating purchase and delivery. These activities can require several separate contacts and steps.

IFEXA GO aims to organise this process in a single digital workflow, connecting online product requests with real-world market sourcing.

## 3. Proposed product differentiator

The proposed differentiator is a **market-aware agent network integrated into the commerce and delivery process**.

The model is designed around four capabilities:

1. **Agent discovery:** customers can identify agents who are available to take sourcing requests.
2. **Market affiliation and proximity:** agent profiles can show the markets they serve and their proximity to the customer, subject to implemented location features and user permissions.
3. **Assisted sourcing and purchasing:** an agent can locate and purchase a requested product on the customer's behalf through a clear approval and payment process.
4. **Sourcing-to-delivery coordination:** the platform is intended to coordinate the request, purchase, and delivery stages, with clear order statuses and accountability.

The intended customer benefit is improved access to physical markets, including when a requested product is not already listed in an online catalogue.

This is a product differentiation hypothesis, not a claim that these capabilities are unique to IFEXA GO. A structured comparison with existing commerce, personal-shopping, sourcing, and delivery services is needed to establish what is genuinely different.

## 4. Intended user journey

The intended workflow to validate is:

1. A customer submits a product request and relevant requirements.
2. The platform presents suitable available agents, including their market affiliation and proximity where supported.
3. The customer selects an agent and agrees on the sourcing and purchasing process.
4. The agent checks product availability and confirms price and any relevant alternatives.
5. The customer authorises the purchase and the applicable payment arrangement.
6. The agent sources and purchases the product.
7. Delivery is coordinated, and the customer receives order-status updates.

This journey describes the intended model. Individual steps should only be represented as implemented after they have been verified in the application.

- How sourcing, order status, and delivery are coordinated.

- Whether the agent workflow addresses a problem not adequately solved by existing alternatives.
